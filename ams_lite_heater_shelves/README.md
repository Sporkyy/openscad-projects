# AMS Lite heater shelves

Stack two SUNLU AMS Lite Heaters near the middle of the workbench in the 3D Printing Room. Each
heater houses a Bambu Lab AMS Lite. One feeds the A1 and the other feeds the A1 mini. Reuse the
surplus nominal 1-inch (25.4 mm) dowels as posts, rails, and shelf slats, joined by printed
fittings.

**Status:** Planning only. The published enclosure dimensions and setup instructions have been
checked. Frame geometry, fit, load, heat, and filament feeding are untested.

## Bench layout and loading access

The workbench has a grey steel frame with adjustable C-channel legs and a 72 × 30 in (1,829 × 762
mm) square-edge maple butcher-block top, 1¾ in (44.5 mm) thick. The matching Global Industrial
listing is model WB183168 with a 2,000 lb bench rating. Measure the installed top height to check
reach to the upper shelf.
[Global Industrial workbench specifications](https://www.globalindustrial.com/p/72-x-30-maple-square-edge-work-bench-adjustable-height-1-3-4-top)

SUNLU lists the heater at 510 × 270 × 265 mm (about 20.1 × 10.6 × 10.4 in) and 3.4 kg (7.5 lb)
before installing the AMS Lite and spools. Stacking takes the closed heater bodies from 1,020 mm
side by side to 510 mm across, before accounting for posts, door swing, or the printers. SUNLU lists
four 1.2 m PTFE tubes per enclosure. Confirm what's in hand for both units.
[SUNLU specifications](https://www.sunlu.com/products/filament-ams-lite-heater) ·
[SUNLU user manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0152/6507/1190/files/AMS_Lite_Heater_user_manuel.pdf?v=1784529650)

- Start with both heater displays facing the front of the bench and the printers on either side.
  Tape out the heaters' closed footprints and mark each printer's bed travel, cable sweep, and
  service space. Check both doors on both tiers through their full swing, including how far they
  drop below the shelf, if any. Leave enough space to pull the largest spool straight off each hub
  and get a hand in to feed it. Set post locations after tracing those paths.
- The closed heater body uses 270 mm of the 762 mm bench depth, leaving 492 mm (19.4 in) to divide
  between the front and rear before adding the frame, plugs, and clearance. Try shifting the rack
  forward or back within the center of the bench if a door or spool overlaps a printer's envelope.
  Check that the upper spools are still easy to reach and the rear tube path stays clear.
- Keep the lower shelf close to the bench while clearing the door sweep and cord. Keep the front
  open so either heater can be removed after cooling and disconnecting. Check that all four spool
  positions on the upper tier are within comfortable reach.
- Try both printer-to-tier assignments with the actual tubes and cables. Compare cable reach and the
  tightest tube bend in each layout. Bambu's stock layout places the AMS Lite to the right of each
  printer and uses four tubes and a four-pin cable per printer. Confirm the alternate routing on
  this bench through full toolhead and bed travel.
  [A1 Combo quick start](https://cdn1.bambulab.com/documentation/quick-start-a75adcb1d5d5e/Quick%20Start%20Guide%20for%20A1%20Combo.pdf)
  ·

## Set the lower shelf headroom from the tubes

Mock up the underside of the upper shelf, including its rails and couplers. As a first trial, place
the lowest obstruction about 365 mm (14.4 in) above the lower heater's support surface, leaving
about 100 mm (4 in) above the published enclosure height before fittings and tubing. The clear
opening must accommodate the installed fittings, a straight tube exit, the sweeping bend of all four
tubes, and room to release the fittings. Measure to the nearest part that crosses that path.

Try turning the lower bundle toward its printer through open space beside or behind the upper shelf.
If a rail or slat crosses the bend, adjust the shelf setback or slat spacing while keeping every
heater foot supported. Raising the shelf is another option if upper-tier reach and stability still
work. Leave the heater vents, plugs, and controls accessible. Exercise the tube bundle at the
toolhead's extremes with the doors closed and check for tight bends, rubbing, and pull at either
end.

## Proposed dowel frame

- Start with four continuous vertical dowels. Each tier has a short front-to-back bearer on the left
  and right, supported at the posts. Seat the printed socket rails on these bearers. Use joints with
  a bearing seat and positive mechanical retention so the rails stay seated when loading or lifting
  the rack.
- Use rear bracing to resist side-to-side racking. Plan fore-aft restraint at the base or rear as
  well, and check tipping with a one-sided loading pull and the upper heater fully loaded. A bench
  attachment or wider base is an open choice. Check the rear edge and underside of the maple top for
  a possible clamp-on restraint. Measure the wall gap and check where the bench hardware leaves room
  for the clamp jaws. Keep braces and fasteners out of the loading sweep and tube route.
- Start with one socket coupon for the measured stock, then prototype one representative joint.
  Favor an upward-opening bore for an axial socket or a two-piece clamp with open saddles for
  horizontal rails. Design both for support-free printing at the repository's 45° limit. Check
  bearing, pullout, and rotation before making a full set. Choose the joint material after checking
  the temperature at its actual location. SUNLU specifies up to 70 °C inside the dryer and calls for
  keeping the vents clear and using a surface that tolerates the heat.
  [SUNLU heater product details](https://store.sunlu.com/products/sunlu-ams-lite-heater-upgraded-your-bambu-lab-ams-lite)

## Shelves made from round dowels

Follow the monitor-riser reference. Run parallel 1-inch dowels left to right and seat their ends in
inward-facing sockets in two low printed side rails. Leave the sides below the slats open for
loading. Check the whole door swing with a mock-up of the outside face of each socket rail and its
bearer, since the doors can swing down past the heater base.

Start with a fairly even row of slats across the shelf depth. Set the final spacing from the
heater's foot contact patches. All feet should sit at the same height and stay seated as the doors
open and spools come off. If a foot rocks or drops into a gap, adjust the adjacent slats or add a
low, flat-topped saddle that keys onto two slats. Keep the underside vents clear.

Fasten the socket rails to the side bearers so the sockets stay engaged. Try a short section with
blind bores printed with the openings facing up. If the full rail is too long for the print bed,
split it into socket sections and fasten each section to the continuous bearer. The slats span the
full heater width in this layout. Measure midspan sag under the loaded footprint before deciding
whether to add a front-to-back center bearer. Check any additional bearer against the lower tube
route and the heater vents.

## TPU feet for the dowel posts

Print four short, truncated-cone feet, one for each dowel that touches the bench. The wide end has a
flat face for traction on the maple top. The narrow end has a blind socket that slips over the
dowel. Seat the squared-off dowel end on the flat floor of the socket so it bears through a solid
TPU pad. Start with a snug push-on fit and enough socket depth to keep the foot seated when the rack
is lifted. Use the [dowel end cap](../dowel_endcap/README.md) as a reference for the socket and the
[foot with post](../foot_with_post/README.md) for printing TPU base-down. Keep the socket open at
the top and use a small 45° chamfer at the bottom edge to allow for first-layer spread.

Use a 40–45 mm base diameter for the first footprint mock-up and keep the pad beneath the dowel
short to limit sideways flex. Mark the full footprint on the bench, check that each foot bears
entirely on the maple, and include the pad thickness when checking door sweep, lower shelf height,
and upper shelf reach. Test traction and tipping separately with ballast. Keep the rear restraint
option open until the loaded frame passes both checks.

## Measurements before modeling

| Measure                                                             | How to take it                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Installed bench height, usable center space, and overhead clearance | Tape measure from the floor and bench edges. Measure the rear wall gap and the clear depth under the rear edge for a possible clamp.                                                                                                      |
| Heater body, feet, doors, and spool access                          | Tape measure or straightedge for the full envelopes and outside-to-outside foot spans across both axes. Use a 6 in caliper for each foot's contact patch. Trace each side's door swing and spool removal, including low points.           |
| Lower PTFE path and connectors                                      | With all four tubes installed, mark the height above the lower heater's support surface where the bundle bends and where it leaves the bay. Measure each route with a tape and confirm both cables reach with service slack.              |
| Dowel stock                                                         | Use a 6 in caliper for OD in two directions at the ends. Measure usable lengths with a tape and count how many full-width slats the stock can supply, allowing for socket engagement at both ends. Check straightness and end squareness. |
| Loaded weight on each shelf                                         | Weigh each heater with its AMS Lite and the heaviest intended set of spools, including the spool reels.                                                                                                                                   |

## Staged verification

- [ ] Mock up the whole bench at full scale. Load and remove spools on both sides of each tier,
      confirm top-tier reach, and check both cable and tube routes through full printer travel.
      Choose shelf positions and tier assignments before cutting stock.
- [ ] Chock the dowels on a low test surface and position them by the heater's actual feet. Mock up
      the side rails at their proposed height and check seating, rocking, door sweep, and spool
      removal. Try a flat saddle if the direct fit needs it.
- [ ] Fit a socket coupon and test a representative joint with the actual dowel, material, and
      retention hardware. Fit a short socket-rail section before making a full rail. Check for
      rocking, rotation, cracks, and pullout. When geometry exists, render the fittings, check mesh
      closure and overhangs, and inspect the previews.
- [ ] Try the same prototype foot on each of the four dowels before printing the set. Check that it
      seats fully and stays on when lifted. Verify the feet's meshes for closure and overhangs.
- [ ] Put the empty frame on the bench. Apply secured ballast at the heater-foot locations at the
      measured service weights. Measure sag at the middle of the width-spanning slats and at the
      side bearers. Check slat roll, joint slip, racking, and tipping with realistic loading pulls.
      Mark reference points and recheck for movement, foot compression, or sag after sustained
      loading.
- [ ] Install one heater at a time. Check vent clearance and temperature at the shelf and joints at
      the hottest intended setting. Test load and unload on all four paths per printer, then repeat
      with both heaters and printers running. Recheck the frame after repeated heating and use.
